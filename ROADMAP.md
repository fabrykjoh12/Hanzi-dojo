# 🗺️ Hanzi Dojo Roadmap

The living plan for what's next. **Editing this file automatically posts an
update to the `#roadmap` channel in Discord** (via `.github/workflows/discord-notify.yml`),
so the community always sees the latest. Keep it current — move things to
**Shipped** as they land.

> **What we're building toward:** *No streaks. No leagues. No guilt. Just real
> progress* — a memory engine, graded stories, and a tutor that only speaks words
> you know, in one calm app. The theme running through the plan below: go deeper on
> reading, add speaking and grammar practice, and let your word knowledge follow
> you everywhere.

## 🚧 Now — in progress

- [x] **Home is a guide now, not a summary.** On a real phone the redesigned Home still read as a dashboard: handsome, and not much help. It is being rebuilt around the actual daily loop — **cards, then a story, then a little practice** — with one rule: only ever *one* of those is the loud thing on the screen. Cards waiting? Cards is the screen, and the story and practice sit quietly below it in order, with the artwork of the chapter you are about to unlock. Finished your cards? Cards shrinks to a ticked line and **the story takes over the screen at the size it was painted** — the covers are wide, cinematic illustrations, and Home has been showing them as a thumbnail the size of a stamp. Read it? Practice steps up, and it recommends the thing that genuinely needs attention rather than pretending to be clever: the words that keep slipping, or the grammar that is due, or nothing at all. **Nothing is invented to fill the sequence** — no chapter unlocked yet says so, a caught-up queue says so, and a day with nothing waiting says *that*, instead of congratulating you for opening the app. Your week's dots and the ten-segment level bar are going: they were informative and they never once changed what to do next. HSK progress stays as one quiet line at the foot of the screen. No points, no streak, no path of bubbles. Three different designs were drawn and rendered on three phone sizes in both themes before picking one, because the last round proved that reading a design in code is not the same as seeing it.
- [ ] **Home's current step is getting something to press.** On the phone the new Home is laid out right and reads flat — correct, calm, and a little lifeless, like a web page rather than an app. The fix is not more on the screen; it is that the *one* thing you are meant to do stops being a coloured rectangle. The main button is becoming a piece of lacquer: light catches its top edge, the lower edge turns away from you, and pressing it compresses the object and takes its shadow away, the way pressing a real thing does. The step you're on gets the drawn object from the bottom bar at a size where it is an object rather than an icon — a card in front of a card on a cards day — because that is the only step with no artwork of its own, which is exactly why it was the flat one. And finishing a day no longer gets a tick in a box, which is the mark every to-do app in the store uses: it gets a **chop**, the vermilion seal with 成 pressed slightly off square, once, and only on a day you actually earned it. Three versions were drawn and rendered at three phone sizes in both themes, twice — the first round's own results threw out half of it, including a numbered marker that turned out to look like a form field. Nothing is being added to the page: same words, same sequence. **And the page no longer stops halfway down.** On a cards day the screen used to end about two-thirds of the way through and leave the rest blank; the three steps now use the height they have, and the space under the button is filled by the one thing that actually explains why cards come first — the artwork of the chapter today's session unlocks, at the size it was painted, with the plain line *finish cards to unlock* beside it. Learn these words, then you get to this. Not a second story card, no badge, no percentage sticker.
- [ ] **The app is getting its own voice, on purpose.** With Home's layout settled, a short style sprint decided the visual language everything else will follow. The rule it serves: **a quiet interface around expressive, meaningful objects** — the story paintings, the seal that closes an earned day, real depth in the controls — instead of every surface getting its own styling. The first candidate was judged cleaner than production and too cautious, so the current one swings back deliberately: **your cards live on one deep-red lacquered panel again** — the app's own hero material, lit from its corner, with a drawn stack of flashcards showing a real word (你好, with its pinyin) and a paper button set into the panel, its arrow in a small red chip. It is the one loud thing on the screen; the story below keeps its cinematic artwork, practice stays a quiet line, and the level progress is one sentence and a hairline. Also decided along the way: a slightly more characterful open-source typeface (in review against the current one at real phone size), crisper corner rounding, and two removals — no welcome-back banner (the gentle easing-in it described still happens; it never needed a banner). **This has now shipped to Home.** Every decision was made from rendered evidence, side by side with the old screen, and the migration was checked the same way: every other screen in the app was measured before and after and came out identical, down to the last text node. The final polish pass is done: the front flashcard became real paper with 你好 printed in the brand's own dark-red ink, the button's arrow moved into a built-in keycap zone instead of a floating circle, pressing it physically settles the button into its own shadow, and the big count switched to proportional figures because the tabular one set the digit 1 adrift. **And then the first build on a real phone sent it back:** the thin line connecting the three steps ran straight through the step numbers, the red panel had grown to half the screen, and the feedback button crowded the level line. The fix is a cleanup, not a rethink — the connecting line is gone entirely (the numbers 1, 2, 3 carry the order on their own), the panel keeps everything and loses about a fifth of its height, the story's painting now runs the full width of the page, practice is one quiet row, and the background wash retreats to the page edges. The reworked screen is rendered and waiting for review; nothing ships until it passes the same phone that failed it.
- [ ] **The red packet is parked.** Your day's cards waiting in a 红包 you open — the idea was drawn, animated on paper and reviewed on a phone three times, and the honest verdict is that a procedurally drawn object is not yet good enough to be the app's signature. The concept is saved whole for a later phase with a properly authored illustration, and nothing decorative replaces it: Home's cards step now makes its case with the number, the words and the button, which turned out to read more confidently anyway.
- [ ] **Making every screen look as considered as the new bottom bar.** The last few months went into the parts you can't see — how the app navigates, how it starts up, how it remembers where you were. That work is done. Now the same attention goes to how it *looks*: one set of spacings, one set of type sizes, one way a button behaves, dark mode that reads properly everywhere, and no screen that still looks like a web page in a phone-shaped window. It starts with an honest audit of every screen rather than a redesign, so nothing that already works gets churned.
- [x] **The app now has one palette, and it is red.** Hanzi Dojo's colour is a deep vermilion — the icon, the wordmark, every hero panel. But its most important buttons were a muted sage green that nobody had ever decided on; it had simply been copied from screen to screen until it was in eleven files. Every one of those is now the brand red, and green means only one thing again: you got it right. The background is warm paper instead of grey-white, dark mode is a warm near-black rather than a blue-black dashboard, and gold is reserved for the moment something unlocks. **Every piece of text in the app was measured against the surface behind it** — the two faintest greys used for dates and captions had been failing readability standards, and one of them was actually *darker* than the label above it, so "faint" and "muted" were the wrong way round. Not a single screen was rearranged: this is the same app in its own colours.
- [x] **One set of buttons, and every one of them big enough to hit.** Under the surface the app had five independent versions of "a primary button and a quiet one", each copied and then drifted — twelve font weights across the app for what is meant to be one style, fourteen different corner roundings on controls, and eight different ways of saying "this is loading". More to the point, roughly half the app's tappable controls were **smaller than the 44-pixel minimum both Apple and the accessibility standards ask for**, and the worst of them were the ones a thumb reaches for most: the close button on a dialog was 28 pixels, the back arrow leaving a story was 30, the reader's own Back was about 26. Those are now one shared set of five controls — a button, an icon button, a list row, a chip and a segmented picker — all of which are at least 44 across, and three of the worst offenders have already been swapped over **without a single pixel changing on screen**: the tappable area grew outward, the picture stayed exactly where it was. Two real readability faults got fixed on the way: a disabled button's label was too faint to read against its own background, and in dark mode white text on the brand red was below the readable threshold on the app's most important control. Nothing has been redesigned — this is the parts bin the next round of visual work is built from.
- [x] **The app is now drawn to one set of numbers.** Under the surface it had twelve font weights, forty-two text sizes, twenty-six corner roundings and forty-four different drop shadows — for a product with, honestly, about four kinds of text and three kinds of box. Most of that was invisible drift: two screens meant to look the same were 0.5px apart because two different people typed two different numbers. It is now five weights, one corner scale, and four shadows. **Two of those were real bugs, not just untidiness.** Every drop shadow in the app was a near-black wash tuned for white paper, which meant that in dark mode most cards had no shadow at all — they just sat flat on the background. And nine of the twelve font weights could never render: only four are actually bundled with the app, so a heading asking for "750" and one asking for "800" were drawing exactly the same pixels while looking different in the code. Nothing was rearranged; a handful of screens shift by a few pixels where a text size moved half a pixel, and the corners are a touch more consistent. Bottom sheets now cast their shadow upward, which is the direction they actually come from.
- [ ] **Fewer boxes, clearer screens — and Home is where you'll see it.** Every section of every screen had grown its own rounded rectangle, so the app was starting to look like a dashboard someone generated rather than something made on purpose. It wasn't the cards themselves: it was the little tinted squares drawn around icons (sixteen of them on the Practice screen alone), the pile of little badges floating over story covers, and the fact that Home used the same grey box for three completely different things — the button that starts your session, the story waiting for you, and your week. Those are gone. **Home now has exactly one card, and it is the one you tap.** The story waiting for you sits underneath it as a real row with the story's own cover art next to it, and your week is just your week: a heading, the seven days, how far you are toward the next HSK level. Nothing was removed — every number that was there is still there. Stories keeps its covers, because that is artwork — and after a round on a real phone, “how much of this story you can read” went back onto the artwork itself, as quiet text along the cover's bottom edge instead of a badge, so the shelf browses like a library rather than a spreadsheet. **And the story on your Home screen actually appears now:** it was asking the database for a column that does not exist, which meant it quietly showed nothing at all for anyone who wasn't midway through a series — since the day it was written.
- [x] **Home looks like the app it is now.** Same screen, same numbers, same one-tap-to-start — but the card panel is a deliberate object rather than a red rectangle: light falling from the top-left corner, a stack of cards embossed into its lower right, and the button you actually press is solid paper instead of the see-through pill it was. That pill was the single weakest thing on the screen: on a phone, where nothing hovers, it never left its faint state. Your daily goal is now ten marks that fill up as you go instead of a sentence about a number. The story waiting for you gets its real cover art at half again the size, with a bar showing how much of it you can already read. Your week is seven small ink dots rather than seven grey slabs — still no streak, still nothing to protect. And progress toward the next HSK level is ten segments instead of one hairline, so "a tenth of the way" looks like a tenth of the way. **Dark mode was designed, not inverted:** the panel lifts to a brighter, warmer red instead of being the same deep plate on a black page. Nothing was added — no points, no path, no extra cards — and the app's text is drawn in five sizes now where Home alone had eleven.
- [x] **The bar at the bottom of the app is a floating tray now, and the icons on it are drawn objects.** This is the first change in the visual pass you can see from across the room. The bar used to run edge to edge, square-cornered, with a frosted-glass blur behind it — a 2018 idea that reads as a film over the screen rather than a thing on it. It is now a solid tray, inset from both sides, rounded on all four corners, floating clear of the bottom edge with the page visible around it. On an iPhone it sits exactly where the home indicator starts, so nothing crowds the gesture area. And the five icons are the drawn set: a training-hall gateway for Home, an open book with a ribbon for Stories, a card in front of a card for Cards, four practice tiles for Practice, three raised slabs for More. The tab you're on lifts into full colour and depth; the rest stay quiet in the same shape, so nothing jumps when you switch. **Nothing about where things are changed** — same five tabs in the same order, Cards still centred and still the biggest, Profile still where it was, Back and the phone's own back button unchanged. Every screen kept its bottom row reachable, and the flashcard screen gave up eight pixels for it, which was measured against the smallest phone the app supports rather than guessed at.
- [ ] **The five icons at the bottom of the app are being drawn properly.** They are currently good outlines — a house, a book, two cards, a grid, three dots — but they are outlines, and next to the rest of the app they look like placeholders. There is now a set of drawn objects instead: a training-hall gateway for Home, an open book with a ribbon in it for Stories, a card in front of a card for Cards, four practice tiles for Practice, and three raised slabs for the More menu. Each one is lit from the same corner, so the row looks like five objects on one desk rather than five icons from five places, and the one you're on lifts into full colour while the rest stay quiet — the same shape either way, so nothing jumps when you switch tabs. Cards stays the most prominent, because it's the thing you came to do, but the first version overdid it: it was more than twice the visual weight of Home, which stops reading as emphasis and starts reading as a mistake. The five are drawn to one weight now, and the bar decides which one is loudest. **The bar itself is not changing** — same five tabs in the same order, Profile still where it is — and there's a sixth icon drawn in the same style for the Profile screen, which is not a tab. **Shipped with the tray above** — the icons and the bar changed together in one go, as planned.
- [ ] **Your profile is being cut back to what it actually tells you.** It had grown into a dashboard: eleven achievement badges (most of them locked and greyed out), a calendar grid of 115 little squares you could tap one at a time, a month recap with a share button, a thirty-bar chart of your review counts, a level-mastery bar, and four separate panels of account settings — four and a half screens of scrolling, and two of those panels used the *same words* for two *different* numbers, so it contradicted itself while you read it. It answers one question in one place now: what level you're on, how many words of it you've learned, how much of it you've mastered, how far the level test is, and how often you've studied lately — plainly, in one panel, with every number labelled by what it actually counts. The badges are gone for good and nothing takes their place: no streaks, no points, no trophies. The calendar is one honest sentence — "studied 12 of the last 30 days" — with no chain to protect and nothing to feel guilty about. The words that keep slipping are still there, five of them, with one button to drill them. Your daily card goal, resetting your Chinese progress and deleting your account are still on Profile, as five compact rows that open when you tap them, with every warning and confirmation exactly as careful as before — and each of them now says exactly what it will do, instead of describing a choice between languages you were never offered. It's about a third of the length it was, nothing you could reach before is unreachable, and there is nothing left on the screen too small to tap.
- [ ] **Practice tells you what to do next instead of handing you eight buttons.** The screen was a two-by-four grid of eight identically-sized drill tiles — Weak words, Grammar review, Listening, Speaking, Writing, Fill in the blank, Sentence builder, Tones, Stroke order — each with an icon in a little tinted square and a one-line description under its name. Eight equal tiles say eight equal choices, which is not true and never was: some of those drills have something waiting for you right now and most of them don't. It opens with one recommendation now — the thing actually waiting, or Listening when nothing is overdue — and the rest sit under it as a plain list you can read in one go. The whole list fits on the screen without scrolling, which the grid never did. Only the drills whose name doesn't explain them carry a line of description, because a description under every single row is what made the screen look generated in the first place; the ones with something waiting show the number instead, as text rather than a badge. **The level test is a quiet line of status now** rather than a card competing with everything else: how many words you've mastered, how many open the test, and a tap to open it. The six places you *look things up* — word list, dictionary, grammar guide, analyze text, videos, and the words you already know — are grouped under their own heading, drawn deliberately quieter, because looking something up is not practising. Nothing was removed: every drill and every tool is still one tap away, and all of them still go exactly where they went before. Two small pieces of wording that had been wrong for a while — "1 pattern are due" — were fixed on the way past.

- [ ] **The introduction now shows you the point before it asks for anything.** It used to open straight onto flashcards; now it opens on a two-line scene from a tea shop, in Chinese, and tells you the truth: *you probably can't read this yet — it takes three words.* Then you learn those three words on the real flashcards, exactly as before — and then you see the **same scene again**, now readable, with the words you just learned marked and translated. Nothing about the text changed; you did. That feeling is the whole product, so the slide at the end that used to explain it in four bullet words is gone — you don't need the loop described after you've just done it. Then one more line appears — **下雨。** — with a word nobody taught you, and the only instruction is *see a word you don't know? tap it.* Tapping it opens the very same word panel you'll get inside any real story: the characters, the pinyin, what it means, a button to hear it. That's the second half of the promise, and it's the one that matters most for actually starting: **you don't have to finish learning before you're allowed to read.** Three smaller things shipped with it: the flashcards now show *when you'll see this word again* under each grade button from the second card on (before, the app said "your grade decides when you see it again" and never showed it); the "session complete" screen now looks exactly like the one you'll meet after every real session, instead of a one-off design; and there's a quiet **Skip** in the corner of every step — skipping takes you straight to creating an account, never back to the sales pitch.

**Just shipped**
- [x] **Three bugs in your first minute are fixed.** The most important one: finishing the introduction and tapping **Create account** used to open the *Log in* form — a leftover from the old signup wizard — so a brand-new learner would type an email and password and be told their account doesn't exist. It now opens the sign-up form, with the password requirement shown, exactly as the button promises. Second: right after the introduction taught you the app, the Home screen would dim itself and explain the same things again with pop-up coach marks — the note that says "this device was already taught" was being thrown away during account setup. It survives now, so the tour only appears for people who skipped the introduction. Third, on Android: pressing your phone's Back button in the middle of the introduction quit the whole app. It now steps back one screen at a time — back through the cards, back to the welcome — and only leaves the app from the very first screen.
- [x] **The bottom bar is finished.** It went through two rounds on a real phone and the second one stuck: **Home · Stories · Cards · Practice · More**, with Cards larger and centred, in a soft panel of its own. It's settled — no more moving it around.
- [x] **The bar at the bottom of the app was redrawn around the thing you actually do.** Flashcards are what you open the app for, so **Cards now sits in the middle of the bar**. Home starts the row, where you'd expect it and where the app opens; Stories sits next to Cards, so the middle of the bar is the language itself; Practice and the More menu are over on the right, which is what they are. Nothing about where the app opens changed: it still starts on Home, and Back still takes you there. **The five icons are drawn for Hanzi Dojo now** rather than picked out of a stock icon set — Cards is two overlapping flashcards, Practice is a small grid of drills instead of a bullseye that looked like it belonged in a sales dashboard, and the tab you're on fills in solid instead of just turning red, so you can tell where you are without relying on colour. The bar is the same height as before and there are no numbers, dots or badges on it: it answers "where do I want to go", and your Home screen answers "how much is waiting". **And the level test came out of the "More" menu**, where it had been sitting between Profile and Log out with nothing in the app linking to it — which meant the one thing standing between you and the next HSK level was the hardest thing in the app to find. It now has a place on Practice, showing plainly how many words you have mastered and how many open it. On top of that the bar had been quietly claiming four pixels more of every screen than it actually used, so the flashcard on a small phone is a little less cramped than it was. **And after a round on a real phone, Cards is unmistakably the main one.** It was still reading as one of five equal buttons: Practice's little grid of drills was actually the boldest mark on the bar, ahead of the tab the whole app is about. Cards is drawn larger now and sits in a soft rounded panel of its own — inside the bar, the same height as before, no floating button and no red block, just the one tab that looks like the thing you came to do. That panel is almost invisible until Cards is the tab you're on, so it never looks like two tabs are selected at once. Practice and More are drawn a touch lighter so they stop competing, Home and Stories are untouched, and the two flashcards in the Cards icon were redrawn upright with clear space between them, so they look like a card in front of a card instead of two overlapping rectangles.
- [x] **Your first minute is the app now, not a questionnaire.** Signing up used to mean nineteen screens: a flashcard mock, a tea-shop story, a completion card, three questions about yourself, an animated "preparing your training path", the account form, a tier grid, a daily-goal picker, a diagram of the daily loop, a welcome screen promising what the next screen would do — and then, once you finally reached Home, four more pop-up coach marks. Two of those questions were asked twice in different words, and four of the answers were thrown away without ever being used. It is now one short interactive tutorial: you learn 你好, 谢谢 and 再见 on the real flashcard, you grade them with the real buttons — which finally say what Again, Hard, Good and Easy actually mean — you watch the session complete, you watch a story unlock, and you read two lines of Chinese made of the three words you just learned. Then, and only then, you're asked to create an account, followed by a single question: are you new to Chinese, do you know some, or do you know your HSK level. Then straight into your real first session. About ninety seconds before the account, twelve taps in total, and nothing you do in the tutorial touches your real progress — those three words are properly yours to learn afterwards.
- [x] **Three things a real phone found.** **A card no longer speaks from behind a screen you're not on.** Leave a half-finished flashcard session, come back to Cards, and the "Unfinished session" offer would appear — while the word from the card you'd had open played out loud behind it. It was the app keeping your session for you a little too literally: returning to the tab re-ran the card's own behaviour even though no card was on screen. Pronunciation now belongs to the card, and only plays when the card is actually in front of you; Replay works exactly as before. **The coloured band across the top of a flashcard stopped glowing in the dark.** It was a colour chosen for white paper, drawn at full strength on a dark card — a cream bar across the top of everything. It's now mixed into the card itself, so it says the same thing (new word / review) far more quietly. Light mode is unchanged, to the pixel. **And the grey scrollbar that rode the right edge of the screen is gone.** It was the web view's own — the last visible sign that the app has a browser inside it — showing up identically on Practice, Stories and everywhere else. Scrolling itself is untouched.
- [x] **The story library now works like the rest of the app.** Stories, a series page and a chapter used to be three faces of one screen quietly swapping itself out — which is why coming back from a chapter sometimes landed you somewhere you hadn't been, and why the shelf you returned to was a fresh one rather than the one you left. They are three real destinations now. Opening a series slides in over the library, and the library is still there underneath, scrolled where you left it and filtered how you left it. Opening a chapter goes properly full-screen. Closing it puts you back on the series you opened it from — not the top of the library, and never Home. Your phone's Back button, the browser's Back button and the on-screen back arrow all now walk the same path, and a link straight to a series opens with the library correctly behind it, so Back has somewhere to go. None of it costs a reload: moving between the three is instant and asks the server nothing.
- [x] **Home stopped reloading itself.** Opening Home used to ask the server twenty-five questions — every time, whether or not anything had changed since you last looked at it. Nine of them were the *same* question asked twice a second apart; four fetched a story recommendation that could never be shown to you if you already had a series going, and one of those pulled the full text of every story at your level to do it. Now Home keeps what it already knew and asks nothing at all when you come back to it: tapping between Home and Stories is instant and completely silent. It still updates the moment something actually changes — grade a card and your queue numbers refresh, finish a session or read a chapter and the story waiting for you updates — but each of those refreshes only what it touched. Grading a card no longer re-downloads your profile. And an app left open overnight catches up on its own: come back to it the next morning and today's numbers are today's, rather than yesterday's until you happened to tap something. Coming back after a few seconds still costs nothing at all.
- [x] **The app keeps its place now, and Back means back.** Underneath every screen, the app was still working like a website: leaving a screen threw it away, so coming back to Stories rebuilt the entire shelf from scratch, asked the server nine questions again, and dropped you at the top of a list you had already scrolled through. The four tabs — Home, Cards, Stories, Practice — now stay exactly where you left them: your scroll position, your filters, what you had typed. Returning to Stories costs no server requests at all. **Back is real.** Every screen's back arrow used to be a shortcut pointing at Home or at Practice no matter where you had actually come from, so opening a word from one place and pressing back sent you somewhere you had never been; there is now one back arrow, in one place, and it steps back up the way you came. Android's hardware Back key follows the same rules — it closes what's open, then steps back, then leaves the app, instead of walking your whole browsing history. **Leaving a flashcard session mid-way no longer throws you to Home**: it pauses on the spot, tells you how many cards you reviewed and how many are left, says plainly that your progress is saved, and offers Resume. **And screens arrive instead of appearing.** A detail screen slides in from the side and leaves the way it came, a full-screen session rises, and switching tabs simply settles — no more replaying the page-load animation every single time you tapped a tab. If you have asked your phone for less motion, none of it moves.
- [x] **The app stops behaving like a web page you downloaded.** A foundation pass over everything underneath the screens — nothing was redesigned, but the app should feel noticeably less like a browser. **It opens in the right colours.** Launching in dark mode used to flash a full white screen before the app caught up, because it always started light and only learned better once your profile arrived from the server; it now knows before the first pixel, and remembers your choice on the device. The phone's own status bar — clock, battery, signal — follows the theme with it, instead of staying dark-on-black. **The launch animation waits for the app instead of a stopwatch.** The ensō used to be dismissed on a fixed 1.4-second timer whether or not anything was ready behind it, so a slow start handed you a half-loaded screen and a fast one held a logo over an app that had been ready for a second. It now hands over the moment there is something real to show — and gives up rather than trapping you if the connection is dead. **The typefaces ship inside the app.** They were being fetched from Google on every launch, so opening the app on a train stalled on someone else's servers, offline meant the hanzi fell back to whatever font your phone happened to have, and every launch pinged a third party. All of it — Inter and the full Chinese face — is now in the app itself: no network, no flash of the wrong font, nothing leaked. **It buzzes, on iPhone too.** Grading a card, switching tabs and finishing a session now give real haptic feedback; iPhones previously got none at all, because the old code used a web API Apple does not implement. Three moments only — this is not a phone that rattles at everything. **Pronunciation plays with the ring switch on silent.** Every spoken word, example and story line was being treated by iOS as incidental web-page sound, so a phone on silent — which is most phones — played nothing, with no explanation. **And the browser tells are gone**: long-pressing a button no longer offers to copy it, dragging across a label no longer paints it blue, and swiping past the end of a screen no longer rubber-bands the whole page. The app is portrait on both platforms now (turning an iPhone sideways used to load the *desktop website* and push text under the notch), and Android's launch screen has proper light and dark backgrounds.
- [x] **The app explains itself now.** New learners were landing on Home after signup with no idea what anything was. There is now a short, calm guided tour — a soft spotlight on one thing at a time, in plain language: this is today's session, this is the story waiting for you after it, this is your week (no streaks to protect), and this is where Stories, Practice and Profile live. The story library gets its own three steps the first time you open it. It runs once, only for a new account, "Skip tour" is always one tap away, and it never appears over a flashcard — the first session already teaches that card by card. You can replay it any time from Settings.
- [x] **Manhua speech balloons hold their words again — and the reader's header stopped being see-through.** On a phone, a line like 你想吃什么？ could sit *outside* its own balloon: the balloons were fixed oval shapes stretched to whatever height the text needed, and an oval pulls its edge in hardest exactly where the first line of text sits — so the taller the balloon, the further the ink pulled away from the words. Worst for a brand-new learner, whose every word carries pinyin and makes each balloon tall.
