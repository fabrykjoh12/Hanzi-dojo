# 🗺️ Hanzi Dojo Roadmap

The living plan for what's next. **Editing this file automatically posts an
update to the `#roadmap` channel in Discord** (via `.github/workflows/discord-notify.yml`),
so the community always sees the latest. Keep it current — move things to
**Shipped** as they land.

> **What we're building toward:** *No streaks. No leagues. No guilt. Just real
> progress* — a memory engine, graded stories, and a tutor that only speaks words
> you know, in one calm app. The theme running through the plan below: go deeper on
> reading, add speaking and grammar practice, and let your word knowledge follow
> you everywhere.

## 🚧 Now — in progress

**Just shipped**
- [x] **The app keeps its place now, and Back means back.** Underneath every screen, the app was still working like a website: leaving a screen threw it away, so coming back to Stories rebuilt the entire shelf from scratch, asked the server nine questions again, and dropped you at the top of a list you had already scrolled through. The four tabs — Home, Cards, Stories, Practice — now stay exactly where you left them: your scroll position, your filters, what you had typed. Returning to Stories costs no server requests at all. **Back is real.** Every screen's back arrow used to be a shortcut pointing at Home or at Practice no matter where you had actually come from, so opening a word from one place and pressing back sent you somewhere you had never been; there is now one back arrow, in one place, and it steps back up the way you came. Android's hardware Back key follows the same rules — it closes what's open, then steps back, then leaves the app, instead of walking your whole browsing history. **Leaving a flashcard session mid-way no longer throws you to Home**: it pauses on the spot, tells you how many cards you reviewed and how many are left, says plainly that your progress is saved, and offers Resume. **And screens arrive instead of appearing.** A detail screen slides in from the side and leaves the way it came, a full-screen session rises, and switching tabs simply settles — no more replaying the page-load animation every single time you tapped a tab. If you have asked your phone for less motion, none of it moves.
- [x] **The app stops behaving like a web page you downloaded.** A foundation pass over everything underneath the screens — nothing was redesigned, but the app should feel noticeably less like a browser. **It opens in the right colours.** Launching in dark mode used to flash a full white screen before the app caught up, because it always started light and only learned better once your profile arrived from the server; it now knows before the first pixel, and remembers your choice on the device. The phone's own status bar — clock, battery, signal — follows the theme with it, instead of staying dark-on-black. **The launch animation waits for the app instead of a stopwatch.** The ensō used to be dismissed on a fixed 1.4-second timer whether or not anything was ready behind it, so a slow start handed you a half-loaded screen and a fast one held a logo over an app that had been ready for a second. It now hands over the moment there is something real to show — and gives up rather than trapping you if the connection is dead. **The typefaces ship inside the app.** They were being fetched from Google on every launch, so opening the app on a train stalled on someone else's servers, offline meant the hanzi fell back to whatever font your phone happened to have, and every launch pinged a third party. All of it — Inter and the full Chinese face — is now in the app itself: no network, no flash of the wrong font, nothing leaked. **It buzzes, on iPhone too.** Grading a card, switching tabs and finishing a session now give real haptic feedback; iPhones previously got none at all, because the old code used a web API Apple does not implement. Three moments only — this is not a phone that rattles at everything. **Pronunciation plays with the ring switch on silent.** Every spoken word, example and story line was being treated by iOS as incidental web-page sound, so a phone on silent — which is most phones — played nothing, with no explanation. **And the browser tells are gone**: long-pressing a button no longer offers to copy it, dragging across a label no longer paints it blue, and swiping past the end of a screen no longer rubber-bands the whole page. The app is portrait on both platforms now (turning an iPhone sideways used to load the *desktop website* and push text under the notch), and Android's launch screen has proper light and dark backgrounds.
- [x] **The app explains itself now.** New learners were landing on Home after signup with no idea what anything was. There is now a short, calm guided tour — a soft spotlight on one thing at a time, in plain language: this is today's session, this is the story waiting for you after it, this is your week (no streaks to protect), and this is where Stories, Practice and Profile live. The story library gets its own three steps the first time you open it. It runs once, only for a new account, "Skip tour" is always one tap away, and it never appears over a flashcard — the first session already teaches that card by card. You can replay it any time from Settings.
- [x] **Manhua speech balloons hold their words again — and the reader's header stopped being see-through.** On a phone, a line like 你想吃什么？ could sit *outside* its own balloon: the balloons were fixed oval shapes stretched to whatever height the text needed, and an oval pulls its edge in hardest exactly where the first line of text sits — so the taller the balloon, the further the ink pulled away from the words. Worst for a brand-new learner, whose every word carries pinyin and makes each balloon tall. Balloons are now drawn to fit the words they actually hold, and a balloon in the gutter is sized by its own line instead of inheriting the width of one meant to sit over the artwork — which is what had been wrapping a six-character sentence into two lonely rows. Separately, the reader's top bar was translucent and stopped below the notch, so artwork scrolled visibly behind the clock; it is now solid and runs to the top of the screen.
- [x] **Stories are a library of series now — and finishing your flashcards unlocks the next chapter.** The whole Stories experience is rebuilt around one loop: study → unlock a chapter → read it → want the next one → study again. The shelf browses like a streaming library — vertical book-style covers, filter chips (All / HSK levels / Manhua), a Continue Reading row, and one hero panel that carries **Today's story reward**: the chapter waiting behind today's flashcard session. Tapping a series opens a proper series page — cover, description, honest progress, one button that always knows where you are (*Start story / Continue chapter 4 / Read again*), a "words you'll recognize" preview drawn from your recent reviews, and a compact chapter list where chapter 1 is always free and later chapters say plainly what opens them: *complete a flashcard session*. Finishing a session shows the chapter you just unlocked with a **Read now** button; finishing a chapter teases the next one and points you back at review. One session = one chapter, no coins, no keys, no streak pressure — the story itself is the reward. Everything you could already read stays readable, and manhua keep their own reader untouched.
- [x] **Your first minute now IS the app: flip a flashcard, then use the word in a story.** Opening Hanzi Dojo for the first time hands you a real flashcard. Tap it and it flips over — 你好, nǐ hǎo, hello, spoken in the app's proper voice. Then you step into a tiny story: Mei walks into a tea shop, the shopkeeper greets her with the word you just learned, and *you* choose what she says back — every option playable aloud before you commit, and a wrong pick just teaches you what that word means instead. When the scene resolves, your first card slides into your collection, you answer three quick questions, watch your training path assemble, and only then are you asked to create an account. If the app closes mid-way, it reopens right where you were.
- [x] **Onboarding is a guided path now — and it ends with you learning your first expression.** Opening the app asks four quick questions — how much Chinese you know, why you're learning, what sounds fun, and how many minutes a day (with the honest math: 10 minutes ≈ 10 new words and a short review). Then it assembles your answers into a visible training path, and before any account exists you complete a real sixty-second first lesson: 你好, spoken by the app's proper studio voice, revealed, and used once in a tiny exchange. Only after *first training complete* does it ask you to save your progress. Every question is skippable and everything can be changed later.
- [x] **The first minute of the app is new — and it no longer needs sound.** Opening the app used to drop you on the website's sales page, which you had already read in the store. Now the ensō draws itself as the app launches, and you land on a calm welcome screen. The "why are you learning Chinese?" step lost its emoji for characters in brush red. And the taste of Chinese before you sign up is a real question now instead of tapping words to hear a robot read them: you guess what 山 means (it's a picture of three peaks), then 火 — and then work out **火山** on your own, having been told neither word. Fire mountain. Volcano. That's the whole method in three taps, it works with your phone on silent, and getting one wrong costs nothing. The app icon is the real brush ensō too, texture and all, instead of the plain circle that was standing in for it.
- [x] **Hanzi Dojo is going to the App Store and Google Play — and the first iOS build is on TestFlight.** The app you use in a browser is becoming a real app on your phone, same product, no rewrite. The iPhone build now goes out end to end without anyone owning a Mac: it compiles on a rented Apple machine, creates its own signing credentials, and delivers itself to TestFlight. Sign in with Apple is in, so is deleting your account from inside the app. Next up is the same pipeline for Android, then testers, then review.
- [x] **The grade buttons explain themselves.** A small "?" next to Again / Hard / Good / Easy opens a plain-language note on what each grade means and what it does to the card's schedule — including that pressing Again loses nothing, it's just how the app finds the right pace. On desktop, hovering any grade button shows the same explanation.
- [x] **Audio that fails now says so — and lets you try again.** When a word's clip doesn't load mid-review (a bad connection, a hiccup), the play button used to go quietly dead for the rest of the card. Every audio control on the flashcards now shows a clear "retry" state instead, and one tap tries the clip again.
- [x] **54 wrong pinyin readings in the HSK 3–6 vocabulary are fixed.** The bulk import behind those levels had let a few defects through: a rare reading winning over the everyday one (厂 showed "hǎn" instead of "chǎng"), a raw "u:" where ü belongs (忽略 read "hū lu:è"), stray capital letters on ordinary words, and missing tone sandhi on words like 一切 and 不必 that HSK 1–2 gets right. All 54 now show the reading you'd actually be taught. (A handful were also *spoken* wrong; their re-recorded audio is still pending — it's waiting on the voice service being reconnected, and it's tracked so it won't be forgotten.)
- [x] **Manhua dialogue is lettered like a comic now.** The old shared component forced every utterance into the same wide rounded rectangle and attached a tooltip triangle, so short lines looked like oversized UI cards and covered more art than they needed. Every manhua now uses content-fitted, slightly irregular ink balloons; curved tails point back toward the speaker; thoughts use a cloud silhouette with three shrinking circles; and narration uses a separate rectangular caption plate. Dialogue wraps into compact natural line blocks instead of balancing itself into extra rows, and the audio/translation tools stack in a narrow column so they no longer force only the first line to break. Long educational lines still move into the gutter rather than covering a face, but spoken lines keep their balloon shape there. The rules come from professional comic-lettering guidance and apply automatically to every existing and future manhua.
- [x] **Stories now feel like a library, not a settings screen.** The shelf uses the familiar streaming-library rhythm: one cinematic featured story, then calm horizontal rows for continuing, top picks, earlier levels, practice, and what unlocks next. The two filter bars, level accordions, long summaries, and piles of metadata are gone; covers are larger, the useful “% known” signal stays, and phone rows swipe without making the page itself overflow.
- [x] **Stories are now ready for a real library, not a demo shelf.** Every one of the 204 published Chinese stories has a cover, English support, a three-question ending check, and line-by-line narration. The shelf opens directly to a story or series with a shareable URL, keeps only your current level open by default, and fits two covers across on a small tablet. Readers now load only when needed, cutting the initial Stories code by about two thirds. Across every story format, controls meet the 44px touch target, dialogs trap keyboard focus, and Chinese text is identified correctly for assistive technology. Manhua still use real speech bubbles, but the disruptive mid-story answer cards are gone everywhere; older saved choices continue safely along the canonical story path.
- [x] **One shelf, sorted for you.** The Stories page no longer hides its books behind First Steps / Growing / Fluent tabs — everything is on one page, in level sections starting at *your* level, and inside each section the stories you can read most of float to the top. Every cover now carries a small **"% known"** pill so you can see at a glance how readable it is before opening it. A whole season is one card: it shows how many chapters you've read, tapping it drops you into the next unread chapter, and a small chip opens the full chapter list. Stories you haven't unlocked yet stay visible with a calm lock and exactly what it takes to open them — and the next level's stories appear at the bottom as the road ahead. The unlock rules themselves didn't change; the shelf just stopped making you dig for what to read next.

- [x] **The app now works properly on a phone — and can be used without sight.** Two full passes over every screen. On phones: in all four story formats the play and next buttons were sitting *below the bottom of the screen* — that's fixed, and the readers now fit the visible window exactly. Around twenty buttons that were too small to hit reliably are now full-size, the tone drill and other cramped grids stack instead of squeezing five things across, small answer text got bigger, pop-up panels no longer hide their last row behind the browser bar, tapping a search box no longer zooms the page, and details that used to appear only when you hovered a mouse — your study calendar's daily counts, a story's full title — are now reachable by tap. For screen-reader users: the practice drills used to say nothing at all when you answered, so whether you were right was carried only by a colour; every drill now speaks the result and the correct answer, Chinese text is finally marked as Chinese (it was being read aloud with an English voice), and dialogs put your focus where it belongs and give it back when they close.
- [x] **The app is honest when the connection isn't.** A full pass over every screen for what happens when loading fails: the app no longer tells you "all caught up" or "no stories yet" when it simply couldn't reach the server — you get a calm "couldn't load — try again" instead, everywhere. A dropped connection at startup can no longer make the app treat you like a brand-new user, the level test finally has a back button (and no more screens you can get stuck on), and a settings change that didn't save now says so instead of quietly undoing itself later.
- [ ] **Public-beta hardening** — a systematic quality pass over the whole app before a wider public beta: tighter account-security guarantees, honest numbers and copy everywhere, editorial sign-off on every published story, accessibility, and speed. Landed so far: the public pages and share cards say what the product is — a Chinese reading app; account boundaries are enforced by the database, not just the interface; the internal metrics were rebuilt so every number means what it says; dictionary search now puts the everyday word first when you search in English (朋友 for "friend", not an obscure classical character); the public reading test tells you its real length instead of a snappy number (the front page now says so too); the site has real **Privacy, Terms, Support and Methodology** pages, linked before signup; and every release now walks a written checklist, with screenshot-comparison tests guarding the key screens against silent layout breakage.
- [ ] **A lot more to read at every level** — HSK 1–6 keep filling up with new serials instead of one season at a time, in 18 different season shapes (mysteries, competitions, a lost-and-found, a send-off, a stray animal) so the arcs stay distinct. The bar to publish went up at the same time: every chapter is scored by a strict editor pass that reads it in the context of the season around it, rewritten while it keeps improving, and drafted from scratch again if it still isn't good enough — anything that never gets there is held back rather than shipped.

- [x] Better-sounding Chinese: new neural voices for every word, example sentence and story line, plus a slow version of each word and sentence for when a word won't stick. Stories now have a narrator and separate character voices.

**Just shipped**
- [x] **The Rainy-Day Noodle Shop — a whole new manhua, three episodes at once, and the first two are HSK 1.** The Inkbound starts at HSK 1 and then climbs, which is no good to you if you started this week. So there is a second drawn series, in warm colour instead of ink, and it stays where you are: a one-counter noodle stall in the rain, a girl called 小美, her mother, and a soaked cat who walks in and decides to stay. Nothing supernatural happens in it at all. **第一话 · 一块钱** — a big man in a wet jacket puts nine coins on the counter for a ten-kuai bowl, and 小美's mother pushes the bowl across without a word about it; he gives the cat half his egg; nobody comments on any of it. **第二话 · 花花** — the rain has stopped, the cat has moved in, and everyone wants to name her except the cat; when the man comes back she walks straight into his arms, which answers a question nobody had asked out loud. **第三话 · 冷了的面条儿** — he eats there every evening until the evening he doesn't, and the cat sits in the doorway watching the street. 小美 already has her coat on. They walk a long wet road to a doorway off an alley, and the bowl they carry has gone completely cold — and he smiles anyway. The first two are **every word HSK 1**; the third is **every word HSK 2**.
- [x] **第三话 · 夜里的字 — and 小白 finally speaks.** 小雨's warning at the end of 第二话 was not a figure of speech. You come back to the dojo at night, and the little ink spirit that has watched you silently for two entire episodes opens its mouth and talks. What it tells you is that the marks come off the page — and then they do, drifting down through the dark hall like slow snow until the whole room is full of them, and they turn to face you all at once, because they remember who wrote them. 林老师 arrives in the doorway completely unsurprised, walks between the tables while the ink parts around him like fish, and holds out his hand. The last thing you see is your own notebook with the bird gone from the page and one ink feather on the floorboards. **Every word HSK 3**, and the plate at the end points at 第四话 at HSK 4.
- [x] **第二话 · 字会说话 — the Inkbound continues, at HSK 2.** The first episode ended by promising you a second one at HSK 2, and here it is: thirty-two lines across twenty-one panels, half again the length of 第一话. You come back early and the hall is empty, but there is already a mark on your table that you did not make — 小雨 says 小白 wrote it, and that the teacher doesn't know. 林老师 sets you one character, a hundred times. Your hand starts to hurt. Then the little ink spirit drifts in and puts a blot straight through the whole page, and what you do about it is the actual lesson: you can shrug and turn it into something, or ask for a fresh notebook and be told there aren't any — either way you end up drawing, and what comes out of the blot is a bird. 林老师 takes the page away without a word about whether it was any good. And on her way out, for the first time since you met her, 小雨 isn't grinning: *don't write alone at night. At night, the characters talk.* **Every word in it is HSK 2**, nothing above your level anywhere, and the plate at the end now points at 第三话 at HSK 3.
- [x] **It's called a manhua now, not a manga.** Same format, right word: 漫画 read the Chinese way is *manhua*, and manga is the Japanese term. The tag on the shelf says **Manhua**, and so does everything behind it.
- [x] **Fixed: the new manhua panels stayed blank even after they shipped.** The app keeps a copy of images on your device so stories still work offline. When a picture didn't exist yet, the server answered with the app's own page instead of a "not found", and the app filed that away *as the picture* — so it kept showing you the page-that-isn't-a-picture long after the real artwork arrived. It now recognises that answer and refuses to keep it, and the old bad copies are cleared automatically the next time you open the app. No need to reinstall anything.
- [x] **第一话 is more than twice as long, and it now tells you where the story goes next.** The first Inkbound episode was ten lines; it is twenty-three, across fourteen panels, and it earns the walk it describes: 小雨 leads you down a lantern-lit walkway, shows you the writing hall, and sits you in front of 林老师, who asks whether you can write Chinese characters — and says something different depending on your answer. Five new panels drawn for it, including the master's brush starting a single stroke. Still **every word HSK 1**, nothing above your level anywhere in it. And the plate at the end now says **第二话 · continues at HSK 2**, so you know both that there's more and what you'll need to read it.
- [x] **The manhua speech bubbles look like speech bubbles now.** They were drawn as true ellipses, which meant a one-line balloon stretched into a huge flat lens that covered half the picture and left its words marooned in the middle of it — worst of all with pinyin turned off, when the balloons are shortest. They're now properly balloon-shaped at any length. The thought bubble lost its dashed outline, which read as "this thing is selected" rather than "this is a thought", and your own chosen reply is marked with a soft tint instead of a bright red ring around it.
- [x] **Fixed: your chosen reply appeared on top of the wrong picture.** After answering 小雨's question, the line you picked was drawn floating over the opening panel of the episode instead of sitting under the choice where you made it.
- [x] **Fixed: the new manhua episode was hiding inside another story's series.** *第一话 · 我是新学生* shipped but never appeared on the shelf. Series on the shelf are worked out from the chapter number in each title, and that only understood the "1." style — a Chinese "第一话" read as no number at all, so the episode was quietly filed as a sixth chapter of *下雪了* instead of standing on its own. Chapter markers written the Chinese way are now understood (第一话, 第十二章, 第10回), so the episode has its own cover on the shelf where you'd expect it — and a future season of them will group together as one series rather than scattering.
- [x] **Hanzi Dojo Stories: read a manhua, and it happens to be a Chinese lesson.** A new kind of story on the shelf — *第一话 · 我是新学生*, the first episode of **The Inkbound**. You scroll a column of full-width ink-and-screentone panels: a lantern-lit village at dusk, a gate that dwarfs you, a girl called 小雨 leaning into the frame to ask whether you're the new student, a teacher in a doorway, and something small and white watching you from behind a lantern. The Chinese is not part of the pictures. Every line is drawn over the artwork as real text, so any word can be tapped for its pinyin, its meaning and its pronunciation without leaving the panel, and the whole line can be played back on its own. Partway through, 小雨 asks you a question and you answer it — say yes, or panic and say no and get a very unimpressed *真的吗？* before the story carries on. Your place, your answer and the words you tapped are all still there when you come back, and the episode only counts as read once you've actually answered and reached the end; scrolling to the bottom isn't reading. **Every single word in it is HSK 1** — there is nothing in the episode above your level at all, and the pictures carry the rest.
- [x] **The grade buttons read as words again.** Again / Hard / Good / Easy had a small icon crammed in beside each label — fine when they sat two-by-two, cramped once all four share one row on a phone. The icons are gone; each button is just its word and the interval underneath it, in the same four colours as before, so the row reads at a glance instead of asking you to parse four little symbols dozens of times a session.
- [x] **A fantasy city, twelve chapters, built on one idea.** *五族城* (HSK 2) — a man who is mediocre at everything decides to build a city on empty land and get five non-human peoples to live in it. Every people's gift and every people's problem are the same fact: the Stone folk live three hundred years and build things that outlast everyone, which also makes them useless in a hurry; the Wind folk fall ill if they stay anywhere past three months, so they are free and nobody trusts them; the Tree folk must plant themselves in one spot and can never travel far from it again, so they are the most loyal and the most trapped; the Night folk cannot go out in daylight, so they guard the city every night of its life and are seen by almost nobody; the Fire folk are dead by forty, so they are brave, and reckless for the same reason. The founder's talent is having none — he can take nothing from anyone, so all five will talk to him, and they will not talk to each other. It runs four hundred years of grudges, one well, a claim to the city that all five can make truthfully and none can share, an offer from a bigger human city to expel the other four and keep the land, and a defence built entirely out of what each people *cannot* do. It is the longest and most deliberately built season on the shelf, and the first written to a lower vocabulary bar on purpose, because the world could not be said otherwise — still 86–92% words you already know.
- [x] **The running serial has covers, and they run too.** Sports-anime key art for all eighteen chapters, with the light doing the arc: HSK 1 is pre-dawn blue on an empty street with one boy in it, HSK 2 goes flat and grey for the injury, and HSK 3 is race-day sun and a road full of people. Two of the pictures rhyme on purpose — an older boy reaching down to pull the hero up off the track after taking his place, and six chapters later the hero on the ground at the finish, reaching up.
- [x] **A running serial, and it is the most shonen thing the vocabulary will allow.** Eighteen chapters, HSK 1 to HSK 3. A boy who comes eighth every single morning says out loud that tomorrow he will be first. He is not first. He gets up at six instead, and the whole story is told in the one thing HSK 1 has plenty of — numbers. His power level is a clock time: twenty-eight, twenty-six, twenty-four, twenty-four, twenty-four. At HSK 1 the language has no word for *fast*, or *win*, or even *run* — so the rivalry is just who reached the school gate first, which turns out to be all a rivalry ever is. At HSK 2 you get 累 and 疼, and the running starts costing something. At HSK 3 you get 比赛 and 坚持, and he finally has the words for the decision. He does not win the last race. He takes ninety seconds off himself and beats the boy who has beaten him every day for two years, by two seconds, and the last thing in it is a hand pulling somebody up off the ground.
- [x] **Sixty new covers, redrawn.** Every story that shipped today has its own illustration, all of them in the same modern register — cinematic, painterly, properly lit, the look of a good animated film rather than a textbook. What changes between seasons is the light: the sea serial runs cold steel blue at HSK 1, deep night at HSK 2 and gold dawn at HSK 3, so the eighteen chapters read as one arc even though you cross two levels to finish them. The empty-building mystery is nearly monochrome and full of rain; the comedy is warm and crowded; the one about five people remembering the same cat is deliberately over-lit and hazy, except for the last picture, which is the only one in focus.
- [x] **A comedy, an argument about money, and a story where everybody is wrong.** Three more seasons, deliberately unlike anything else on the shelf. *一个字* (HSK 3) is a farce whose entire plot is one character: a boy writes 卖 where he meant 买, and by Thursday half the street has come to say goodbye to a noodle shop that was never closing. *对面的新店* (HSK 5) is what happens when a chain opens across the road from a thirty-year-old shop — it does not end in winning, because that is not what happens. *大毛是从哪儿来的* (HSK 6) asks five people where the street cat came from and gets five answers that cannot all be true, and three of them remember being the one who fed him first.
- [x] **The emptiest shelf in the app is no longer empty.** Once you were thirty words into HSK 2 there was, until now, nothing at all written for you — that tier had zero stories. It has six: *天天来的那只狗*, in which a dog turns up outside the shop at four o'clock every afternoon, sits, and leaves at five, and Li Ming works out that dogs do not own watches. Following it home takes twenty minutes on foot and answers a different question than the one he asked.
- [x] **A mystery, which the shelf did not have.** *五楼的灯* — somebody is sweeping the stairs of a building nobody has lived in for two years. Six chapters of evidence, and the answer at the top of the fifth flight is a person, not a ghost. Six new chapters at HSK 4, in a shape none of the other seasons have.
- [x] **One story that grows with you: eighteen chapters running from HSK 1 to HSK 3.** A boy hides on a ship, is put to work counting its water, and works out that the number is wrong. Six chapters at HSK 1, six at HSK 2, six at HSK 3 — and the story deliberately can only tell you as much as your level can say. At HSK 1 nobody explains anything, because HSK 1 has no word for *because*; every adult answers with silence and the whole mystery is arithmetic. At HSK 2 people start giving reasons, mostly wrong ones. At HSK 3 the language can finally hold a past — and that is the chapter where you find out what the crew did three years ago, and why none of them would say it. Reading it end to end means climbing two levels, and it is written so that is the point.
- [x] **Twelve new HSK 1 chapters — two full seasons at the very start of the ladder.** HSK 1 had the shortest reading list in the app at the two tiers you reach after your first hundred words. Now there is *我做晚饭*, six chapters in which Li Ming decides he can cook his mother one dinner and is wrong about that four times before he is right; and *店外边的椅子*, six chapters in which the white cat that sits outside the shop every day stops turning up, and the woman who has sat beside it for years turns out to know exactly where it is. Both are written entirely inside the HSK 1 word list — **100% of the words in every chapter are already on yours**, with nothing to look up and nothing skipped over.
- [x] **Grading a flashcard on a phone no longer means scrolling.** After revealing a card you had to scroll down to reach Again / Hard / Good / Easy — every card, dozens of times a session. The study screen now fits one screen exactly: the card takes whatever room is left and scrolls its own example sentence if a word has a long one, while the four buttons stay put where your thumb already is. Desktop is unchanged.
